The Model in One Screen

We're a marketing-and-sales company that installs water systems — not a plumbing company. Generate cheap leads, close them fast on the phone, install below what Culligan charges, and win on volume. You don't need to be a plumber. You need a lead engine, a sales process, enough product knowledge to prescribe right, and a compliant way to install.

The wedge — why this works

The market splits into two camps with a wide gap between them. We own the gap.

🏚️ Mom-and-pop shops
30+ years, word-of-mouth, little internet presence, often poor service. Cheap-ish but invisible and unprofessional.
🏢 Big dealers
Culligan, Kinetico, EcoWater, Puronics, Leaf Home. Polished and trusted — but $5K–$12K, built on 2-hour kitchen-table hard-sells.
★ Our positionMore professional, more visible, and a better experience than the mom-and-pops; dramatically cheaper and far less painful than the big dogs. The decisive edge: almost nobody in this space runs paid ads. A competent ad operator owns the digital channel by default.

Pick a side — never the middle

You're the cheap, high-volume operator (undercut the big brands, close fast, win on volume — this is us). The other valid model is the premium in-home seller at $7K–$12K. What kills operators is getting stuck in the middle at $4K–$5K: too expensive to phone-close, not enough margin for a premium motion.

Same strategy, two anglesPhone-close and low pricing are the same thing. No windshield time, no paying reps to drive → lower cost structure → which is exactly what lets us price below everyone.

The Phone-Close Walkthrough

The most counterintuitive fact about this model: you can sell a $3,000 softener over the phone — no in-home test, no kitchen-table demo. A clean city-water close runs ~4–5 minutes on a fixed rhythm. Internalize the structure, not the words.

Why phone-close worksThe lead is already in-market (they opted into your offer ad) · the price is already attractive (you undercut the $8K–$12K big-box quote) · you drop the price up front (kills friction before it's an objection) · relentless follow-up closes the rest.

The seven beats

1
Confirm intent in one line
"I got your request for info on a water system — still something you're in the market for?" Re-opt-in; don't re-sell what the ad already sold.
2
The five-question qualify
City or well → people in the home → bathrooms/sq ft → 110V outlet under the kitchen sink → where's the shut-off valve. These five answers prescribe city water correctly. (Optional rapport: "one of the new communities?" — new builds are the honey hole.)
3
Turn "garage shut-off" into a gift
If it's in the garage: "Perfect — that makes the install so easy we don't even charge for it." A free install framed as a consequence of their good luck, not a discount you're begging them to take.
4
Brochure over text, walked through live
Text the cropped brochure (never a raw link), narrate while they look: carbon tank, resin + salt does the softening, gravel holds it, RO on the right, "we color-match the second faucet to your kitchen." Name parts briefly — credibility, not a chemistry lecture.
5
State the all-in price once, flat, with conviction
"All in — system, taxes, install — your total is $2,999. You'll never pay a dollar more." Then immediately: "Any questions or concerns I can address while I've got you?" Don't trail off — hand them the mic.
6
Volunteer warranty + maintenance as trust, not defense
Don't wait to be asked. "Lifetime on the valve and tanks, 5 years on the media, 1 year on the RO — after year one it's just a small trip charge." Then the salt reassurance. Proactive warranty-talk removes the fear before it forms.
7
Close = book the install, then capture logistics
"When are you looking? I've got a 10–12, 12–2, or 2–4 most days." Once they pick: photo of the shut-off valve + photo of the kitchen faucet (color-match), address, payment method. "You won't pay me a thing over the phone — you pay the tech once it's done." Booking the install IS the close.

Two pricing-objection pre-empts worth stealing

The "it's just an appliance" disarm"Honestly these are pretty simple systems — it's just an appliance at the end of the day. We don't feel right making a ton off one, so we found a way to keep the cost low without ever sacrificing quality." Justifies the low price and signals honesty.
The "call a thousand companies" line"You could call a thousand water companies and try to beat this price — I'm confident we're at or very near the best, and we never cut quality to get there." Invites the comparison shop you'll win, which kills the urge to do it.

Rules that carry the close

  • Multiple decision-makers: for $3K+, get both on the same call (phone/FaceTime) before you present price. "Anyone else part of this decision? Let's grab them so I only walk through it once."
  • Shoot your shot every call. Never hang up without trying to book or get a yes/no. "What do you need from me to make this happen?" A fast no is a gift.
  • Book a meeting from a meeting (Bam-Bam). Never end a call without the next contact time set.
  • The low-pressure educational sell: your price already pre-qualified them. Diagnose, prescribe, reassure — it plays as relationship-building, not selling.
  • The 5% who go deep: "Sounds like you know more about this than I do — I'm the owner, not the master plumber who'll do your install. Let me get exact answers and call you right back." Then check the FAQ bank and close.
Lifetime-support group chat (sold as a feature)Before install, make a group chat with the customer, lead installer, and office/CS: "this is your direct line to us for anything, forever." It kills the new-company-longevity worry, runs the install confirmation, and becomes the annual RO-filter reminder thread.

The 5-Question Qualify

These five answers are all you need to prescribe correctly on city water. Ask them early, conversationally, in the discovery call. Don't skip the last two — they prevent the two most common install-day disasters.

1
City or well?
City = one-size-fits-all, phone-close it. Well = case-by-case, slow down (see Well-Water Logic).
2
How many people in the home?
Sizes the system. The first three questions decide single vs. XL vs. dual. Don't mis-size a big house; don't oversize a small one.
3
How many bathrooms / approx. square footage?
Over 4 bathrooms and over 6 people → default to the Dual-Tank (city). We size off the home, not water hardness. The bigger setup is about longevity, not flow.
4
Is there a 110V outlet under the kitchen sink?
Sets the RO type. Tankless needs power; tank-style doesn't. Confirm here — don't promise tankless to a home with no under-sink power and discover it on install day.
5
Where's your main water shut-off valve?
Double duty: tells you install difficulty and sets up the "garage = free install" line. Garage/accessible = easy. Street meter only = possible trenching (flag a range).
Optional fast rapport-qualifier"One of the new communities?" — new builds are your honey hole and budget-flush (just closed, motivated).

FAQ Bank

Keep this open and search it live mid-call. Proven customer-facing language for the technical questions you'll actually get. Use the search box in the sidebar, or click to expand.

? How do I maintain it / the salt?▶
Two analogies that land: "Treat the brine tank like a gas tank — fine to let it get low, you just never want it to hit empty or you'll lose water pressure." And: "It's like a trash can — pop the lid, glance in; the tank's black, the salt's white, easy to see. Under about half, grab another bag."

Specifics: Home Depot, Diamond Crystal, ~$8/bag, one bag at a time. First fill lasts ~2 months, then every ~2–3 months for a family of four. Check every ~6 weeks until they learn their rhythm.
? Can I buy the RO filters myself?▶
"No — those come through us." Said plainly, without apology (this is the recurring lock-in). Then reassure: at the ~1-year mark the support group chat pings them — "ship them to your door or send a tech, same price either way, currently $199, covers all three filters."
? Will I lose water pressure?▶
No — the system is built around a wide 1.25" distributor tube and ~28 gpm flow, far above what a home draws (~10 gpm max). The only pressure-loss scenario is letting the brine tank hit empty (gas-tank analogy).
? Is the RO water too pure / stripped of minerals?▶
Good instinct — straight RO removes nearly everything, so we add a remineralization (alkaline) filter: "the healthiest, best-tasting water you can put in your body," with the minerals back in. (Agree with the customer first, then solve it.)
? I only tested for chlorine — why a softener?▶
Don't argue them into a softener — give them the honest economics and let them choose: "You don't have to — our carbon-only filter handles chlorine, taste and odor at $1,999. For about $1,000 more, the Complete Home adds the resin that actually softens your water — no scale, better skin and hair, longer-lasting appliances — same install either way." The mixed bed removes chlorine identically to carbon-only, so the only question is whether soft water is worth the ~$1,000 step up. Low-pressure framing protects the relationship and usually lands the softener.
? I don't want to deal with refilling salt.▶
When the resistance is specifically the salt chore (not price), pivot to the Salt-Free Complete System ($3,599) — "no salt, no brine tank, no drain, nothing to refill." Be honest (per the lineup): it conditions/reduces scale but doesn't make truly soft water and has no health benefit. Since you offer both, present it straight and let them choose — it protects the ticket instead of caving on price.
? How long does the system last?▶
~7–12 years (5 in heavy-use/bad-water homes, up to ~20 for light users). When the media is spent we don't sell a whole new system — we swap the tank and the lifetime-warranty valve stays. Quick and far cheaper than a full reinstall.
? Is the salt-free system as good?▶
Be honest: salt-free conditions (reduces scale on plumbing/appliances) but doesn't produce truly soft water and has no health/heavy-metal benefit. ~99% of customers choose salt once it's explained. Because we offer both, we can be the unbiased one.
Why the salt system is better for your health — keep it this simpleThe salt system actually removes things from your water. The salt-free doesn't. Salt softening pulls the hardness minerals out — and small amounts of certain metals come out with them — so you get genuinely cleaner, truly soft water. The salt-free system removes nothing; it just keeps those minerals from sticking to your pipes, so everything's still in the water. Both cut down on buildup — but only the salt one actually takes things out. That's the honest difference; then let them decide.
? Is it NSF certified?▶
Yes — NSF-certified on every component that touches water (valve, tank, carbon, resin, gravel), not one vague whole-unit cert. Keep the per-line-item NSF links handy — it's a strong trust close.
? Does it remove PFAS / forever chemicals?▶
The RO at the kitchen removes ~99% of PFAS at the tap — a rising, emotionally potent concern (watch for local contamination news, e.g. 3M-type settlements). Whole-home carbon helps too; the RO is the answer for drinking water.
? Can you hook it to my fridge/ice maker?▶
Newer fridges communicate poorly with RO pressure — generally talk customers out of it (or charge ~$300+ and set expectations). A kitchen island often makes routing impossible without tearing up flooring.
? Where does the drain/backwash water go?▶
We typically run the drain out to the yard rather than tying into septic/sewer. Reassure in plain terms: "it pumps out the water just once a month around 2am, and it'll likely be all absorbed before you even wake up." Disclose up front so it's never a surprise. (Confirm local code where it matters.)
Build the bank as you goEvery new technical question + its answer gets added here so every rep can search it live. This is the single Google-Doc discipline that handles technical objections without anyone being a water expert.

Diagnose & Prescribe

Customer water complaints almost always reduce to four things. Know what each is and how your system fixes it, and you can prescribe a solution to virtually any city-water caller without a parts lecture. Sell the outcome, not the chemistry.

The "Big Four"

💎 Hardness
Scale, dry skin & hair, appliance wear.
→ Softener
🧪 Contaminants / chlorine / taste
Chemical taste & odor.
→ Carbon (whole-home) + RO for drinking
☣️ PFAS / forever chemicals
Rising, emotional concern — watch local news.
→ RO removes ~99% at the tap
🔩 Heavy metals (incl. iron)
Staining; heavy iron is a well specialty case.
→ Softener/filtration + RO
★ The standard prescription for the 95%Whole-home softener (carbon + gravel + resin/salt, mixed-bed for city) + tankless RO at the kitchen for drinking. The RO is a near-no-brainer add: "we're already in the home, it's only ~$400 more, takes up no space, and gives you the best-tasting, purest water you can get."

Outcome-based discovery

If they don't volunteer the problem, ask for the desired outcome, not the symptom:

Ask"Are you mainly after softer water for your hair and skin, better-tasting drinking water, longer-lasting appliances / less scale — or all of it?"

Whatever they pick, you're prescribing the same core solution (softener + RO) — but now you mirror their own stated goal back when you present, which closes far better than a parts lecture.

What to actually say (sell the outcome)

The pitch"Your skin and hair will feel great, your water will taste amazing, your appliances last longer, and ~99.9% of contaminants, PFAS and heavy metals are gone." Not: a lecture on ion exchange.
Local micro-knowledge = instant credibilityKnowing neighboring towns' water profiles is one of the fastest trust signals on a call. Have a city/ZIP water fact ready to drop. You already have this — your Water Report tool covers 98 metro cities; pull the specific number before the call.

Speed-to-Lead & Follow-Up

The statistic the operators live by: response odds drop ~90% after the first 60 seconds. Call immediately on inbound. Then out-follow-up everyone — most deals close on the 7th–12th touch.

First contact — multi-channel, every time

One "touch" =double-dial + voicemail + text — ideally from an iPhone so it lands as iMessage and a voice note. A FaceTime-audio call from a personal cell dramatically lifts pickup (nobody gets a sales FaceTime, so they answer).

The follow-up sequence

  • Day one: 3 touches (morning / afternoon / evening), then daily.
  • Most deals close on the 7th–12th touch (some say 6–8). "No response" means "not yet," not "no."
  • Pattern their availability — working people answer evenings.
  • Update CRM notes after every contact.
  • After ~7–8 failed touches → move to the flash-sale bucket for periodic mass-offer campaigns.

The friction re-engagement text (for ghosters)

Send"I've been chatting with you about a water softener and you haven't responded — are you still serious about this, or should I stop reaching out?"

Bluntly offering to stop re-engages a surprising share. (One operator: 8 sent, 3 came back — some who'd never replied at all.)

The Flash Sale / Reactivation Campaign

Never discard unconverted leadsPool them, then ~monthly — anchored to any holiday or invented end-of-month deadline — blast a limited-time offer (a few hundred off, a free add-on) via mass text + email. One documented case: ~40 sales / ~$95K in a single Black Friday weekend from reactivation. The flash-sale bucket is found money.
iPhone vs. CRM (current practice)Run daily lead comms on a real iPhone / eSIM line (iMessage lands, voice notes + selfie videos lift response) and keep the CRM for organization, instant new-lead notifications, and mass texts. GHL green SMS reads as "scam likely" and gets filtered. A second eSIM line (~$10–$15/mo, local area code) beats third-party texting tools.

System Lineup

What you'll actually sell, and the credibility story behind each. You don't need to be a water expert — this is back-pocket knowledge, not your pitch.

Large — Single-Tank Mixed-Bed

99% Workhorse · $2,999

A single tank with ~1.5 cu ft resin + ~0.5 cu ft carbon (matched so both expire together). Removes hardness + chlorine. What you sell on the vast majority of city jobs. If they push "single tanks don't last as long" — honest answer: it's not worse water, it just doesn't last as long as a dual (~7–12 yrs vs. ~12–20), and we offer both so we can be unbiased.

Extra Large — XL Single-Tank

$3,399

Same system, larger tank (~64,000-grain). An optional step-up for larger homes — but for a big home (over 4 baths and over 6 people) our default is the Dual-Tank, not the XL. Flow is essentially the same (~28 gpm) — the bigger tank is about longevity. Don't special-order an XL when the standard will do.

Dual-Tank City — The Premium

Best City System · $3,799

Two tanks (carbon + resin) with the patented ability to backwash the carbon tank too — so it self-cleans and lasts 10–20+ years instead of needing a carbon swap every ~2–3 years. ~$800 over the Large while your added cost is only a few hundred — an easy, honest upsell. Frame it straight: same water quality, just lasts longer; worth it if they're staying long-term.

Carbon-Only Filter

$1,999

Whole-home carbon removing chlorine, chemicals, taste/odor without softening (no resin, no salt, no brine tank). Low build cost (~$300), so at $1,999 it lands right at the ~$1,000 floor — and it's ~$1,000 under the Complete Home softener, so the resin/softening is the natural ~$1,000 upsell. Prescribe only when the customer genuinely wants filtration without softening.

Salt-Free — Conditioner & Complete System

Honest framing required · $2,599 / $3,599

Built like Puronics units (same NAC media) at a fraction of their ~$6K–$7K. No salt, brine, drain, or electricity. Two ways to sell it: the conditioner alone at $2,599, or the Complete System at $3,599 which adds the carbon tank for chlorine removal (NAC can't be mixed with carbon, so it needs its own tank). The honest truth: it does not make truly soft water, has no health/heavy-metal benefit, and only reduces scale. ~99% choose salt once explained — but offering both makes you the unbiased one. Media lasts ~3–5 years.

Dual-Tank Well (AIO)

Well default · $3,999

For well water with iron ≤ 7 ppm. An air-injection downflow valve injects oxygen, converting iron to rust that's filtered out; handles iron, minor sulfur, manganese. The default well system. Never guarantee 100% removal — set expectations.

Hydrogen-Peroxide Injection

Heaviest well · $5,999

For iron > 7 ppm, strong rotten-egg smell, or slimy film in the toilet tank (bacteria sign). A peroxide pump draws from a ~5-gal jug (customer refills, ~$70/jug) and oxidizes through a carbon tank before softening. Big-box charges $9.5K–$15K.

Reverse Osmosis — Drinking Water

$599–$699

Tankless (preferred): better looking, ~50% less under-sink space, DIY-friendly filter changes, proprietary filters = recurring lock-in. Needs a 110V outlet under the sink. Often built with a remineralization filter. Tank-style: slightly better water quality but bulkier, more leak points, needs a tech for filters, add alkaline separately (~$25). Better for well (higher TDS / lower pressure).

The components (credibility story)

Ceramic-disc valve — lifetime warranty US-made 10% cross-linked resin Coconut-shell activated carbon 1.25" distributor tube — no pressure drop NSF on every wetted component 110–120V standard plug
The differentiator storyThe valve (~40–50% of unit cost) is the brain — a ceramic disc that essentially never wears out (vs. plastic-piston valves competitors service every 2–5 years), which is why a true lifetime valve warranty is possible. City water is one-size-fits-all. Well water is the one genuinely case-by-case area — slow down here. Ask the customer to list every complaint, and test (or get their lab results). You're really testing for hardness, iron, and nitrates.

Install Variables That Affect Price

The advertised price assumes a normal install. Always ask where the main shut-off is. Quote a range or flag it when these come up — and have the installer walk the customer through any change before he cuts anything.

🚧 Trenching
No accessible shut-off / only a street meter → possible trench from street to garage. The big variable — get a per-foot/scenario sheet from your installer. Target ≤ ~$1,000 for a loop, not $1,500–$2,000+.
💎 Granite/quartz drilling
Standard with a ½"–⅝" diamond bit (kept wet, 45° start). ~$200 to buff a rare scratch. Don't let installers tack on $150/hole "liability" charges — it's routine.
🔌 Under-sink power (tankless RO)
Confirm in discovery — don't discover it on install day. No outlet + no easy tap → sell the tank unit on its merits, or ~$200 electrician (garbage-disposal-button tap, outlet splitter, fish a line).
🚿 Drain line
The other big time variable (10 min to 2 hrs). Default: run to the yard. "Pumps out once a month ~2am, absorbed before you wake." Disclose up front. Confirm local code where it matters.
🪠 Galvanized / old pipes
Softened water can leach — flag as a known issue, don't promise around it.
🎛️ Valve / control-head siting
An experienced installer may ask if the head goes under the cabinet or in the garage/crawlspace. Know your config so you can answer (also a signal the installer knows the product).
❄️ Outside installs / freeze
Fiberglass tanks are fine outdoors; in hard freezes tell the customer to run a slow drip. Weather damage is never warranty-covered ("act of God").
Quote honestly"Best case it's our advertised price; worst case, if there's a long trench or a surprise in the attic, it could go up to ~$X — and our installer walks you through it before he cuts anything."
Responsibility split (drain line)The installer makes the reasonable on-site routing call and owns workmanship. The operator owns flagging a customer's stated drain preference in job notes before the install. A clean routing the customer was never warned about is on the operator; sloppy workmanship is on the installer.

Complaint Resolution

Complaints cluster around three things: install quality (leaks/workmanship), system performance ("still feels hard"), and service responsiveness. Handle them fast — a water complaint can become a water-damage complaint, and reviews are the lifeblood of your free-lead engine.

Principles

  • Speed and ownership — the same speed-to-lead reflex applies to complaints; it protects the review profile.
  • Most issues trace to the installer, and they're rare (<5% warranty-claim rates). The claims that happen almost always come from new/unfamiliar installers (especially programming errors). The fix for chronic issues is the crew, not the customer.
  • Performance "complaints" are usually expectation gaps — a softener doesn't remove iron/lead; salt-free isn't truly soft; UV doesn't make well water drinkable; an RO isn't a softener. Set correct expectations at the sale so these drop.
  • Negligence (no salt → resin "fails"): resin doesn't fail in the first ~60 days unless they never add salt. Hard to prove (they add salt right before you arrive) — a soft argument for the service-plan touchpoint.
  • Weather: freeze damage is never warranty-covered ("act of God") — but proactively mass-text customers before freezes to run a slow drip. Cuts call-ins, earns goodwill.
  • Protect margin, but protect the review more. Fix/adjust rather than refund for fixable issues; reserve refunds for genuinely unsalvageable cases. Escalate rep → sales manager → owner — don't let a setter or installer freelance a high-stakes resolution.
The warranty model (your shock-absorber)Lifetime: valve + fiberglass tank + brine tank. 5 years: electronics + media. After 12 months, any service is a flat trip charge (~$149–$249) — this kills replacement-part sticker shock. Anything promising media/electronics beyond 5 years is a red flag. On a genuine manufacturing defect, the supplier ships replacement equipment free; the dealer eats the install labor (that's why you keep ~$1,000 margin per job).
Vet installers hardPrefer someone who's done 25+ softener installs; meet them face-to-face if you can. Make water-test-before-and-after with photos an installer SOP, and have them watch the system-specific install video (dual-tank especially) before the job. (One horror story: an unvetted installer's improper drain-line connection flooded an attic for ~$40K.)

Licensing, Insurance & Compliance

Read this before your first install. This is the one vertical where install legality is regulated, varies by state, and can genuinely sink an operator. Water treatment touches a home's potable plumbing, so many jurisdictions regulate who may legally perform the install.

⚠ Not legal adviceLicensing, plumbing-code, and contractor-classification rules vary by state/locality and change. Confirm with your state licensing board and a local attorney/CPA. The operator is responsible for compliance. For Minnesota specifically, confirm current rules with the MN Dept. of Labor & Industry / Plumbing Board before your first install.
The enforcement trapOperators will tell you, accurately, that permits are rarely pulled for softener installs and enforcement is often light. That lived reality is real — and it is precisely the trap. The ones who get burned assumed "nobody checks" right up until a flooded attic, a failed inspection during an unrelated remodel, or a worker-classification audit. The downside is asymmetric: years of "fine" can be erased by one $40K water-damage claim.

The checklist before operating in a market

  • Does your state require a license to install whole-home systems?
  • Can a non-licensed owner run the business while licensed installers do the regulated work? (Often yes — the standard structure.)
  • Are point-of-use ROs treated more leniently than whole-home? (Often yes.)
  • Are permits / inspections required? Build their time + cost into pricing.
  • Is the install work done by genuinely-licensed plumbers/installers where the law requires? (Doing regulated work through unlicensed labor is where operators get exposed.)
  • Is each installer correctly classified? Paying per-job doesn't make someone a valid 1099 — if you control schedule, methods, and tools, the IRS/state may reclassify (back taxes, penalties).
  • General liability on the business; installers carry their own GL ($1M min), name you as additionally insured.
  • Written sub agreement that damage is the sub's responsibility first (but the customer comes after you first — your coverage is what protects you).
  • Sales/use tax on installed equipment — depends on billing model (lump-sum vs. separately-stated). Confirm with a CPA / your state DOR.
⚠ Insurance is non-negotiableWater work has real water-damage exposure (one documented case: a single bad drain-line connection caused a ~$40K loss). Don't operate uninsured; don't let an uninsured sub touch a customer's plumbing. The insurance-audit trap: insurers price on projected revenue — if you tell them $120K and do $10M, an audit hits you with a large back-charge. If you'll exceed projected revenue, tell your carrier immediately (raising coverage often costs surprisingly little).
Bottom lineWhen considering a new state or sub structure, always surface the plumbing-license question and the 1099-classification question, and confirm with the state board + a local attorney/CPA. Never assume a structure is "fine" or skip permits/licensing because "no one checks."

Hiring & Scaling

The whole game is replacing yourself, in order: labor → sales → admin → leadership. You cannot scale if you do every job. Prove someone else can do what you do, then delegate fast.

Phase 1 — Replace yourself in labor (first hire = installer)

  • Trigger: you're still doing installs. Replace that first. (Don't hire a second installer until consistently ~7 days booked out — hire on demand, never hope.)
  • Hire a 1099 per-job installer (~$400/install; post a $300–$750 range). Own truck/tools/insurance. Prefer someone with ~20–25+ filtration/softener installs (a hard screen — "I've done one or two but I'm a quick learner" is not enough).
  • Profile: experienced and hungry; skew younger (~50 and under). People coming off big install mills (Leaf Home Water, etc.) are gold.
  • Where: Indeed (sponsored ~$65–$110/day for ~3 days, then call applicants like hot leads) · Facebook/Nextdoor (be blunt — contractors respect directness) · Home Depot "Pathway to Pro" (free directory) · direct cold-pitch (ask non-fits to refer — plumbers know plumbers).
The installer pitch (you're recruiting AND retaining)"All we do is whole-home softeners + tankless RO — nothing else, ever." (Narrow scope = professionalism.) "I handle sales, marketing, invoicing, materials, payment, logistics — you bring labor and a truck. $400 a job, 3–5 a week — that's ~$1,500/week onto your books with zero overhead, ramping toward 5+ as we grow. I'd rather work closely with a few people doing volume than juggle twenty — you'd be my third." Then the trust ladder: first job or two you meet on-site; after that, a storage-unit code. "I make your life easy, you make mine easy."
Non-branded clothing until shirts arriveTell subs to wear plain clothing until you've given them your branded shirts — homeowners get confused (and trust dips) when the installer shows up in another company's branding.

Phase 2 — Replace yourself in sales (first rep)

  • Don't hire a rep until you can feed ~100 leads/month (≥150–180 once ramped). Delegate ≤50% of leads at first.
  • Comp evolves from 1099 commission-only → W2 salary + commission once proven (the single biggest payroll lesson — one operation cut payroll ~$85K→$40K/week at the same volume). Recommended: ~$750/week salary + ~$50–$100/sale (escalating % over a floor) + ~$500 bonus for 10+ installs/week.
  • Profile: hunger and cold-calling stamina over water experience. Best backgrounds: life insurance, solar, real estate/wholesaling, B2B. Avoid over-qualified managers; avoid poaching a competing local water dealer.
  • Don't read a new rep's KPIs for 1–2 months. Ramp: month 1 ~5% close, month 2 ~10%, then ~15%+.

Phases 3–4 — Admin, then leadership

  • Admin: first admin can be remote/from-home (trustworthiness > pedigree). Hand off morning-of invoicing, AR/collections, confirmations, support, inventory, the master tracker. A warehouse/service person who divvies inventory + does filter changes is a common early double-hat.
  • Leadership: lead installer, sales manager (promote your top closer), office manager owning ops. One comp plan per role.
  • Structure to exit from early: bookkeeper (~$750/mo) from day one, S-Corp, reasonable W-2 salary, reinvest aggressively. Water treatment trades at ~6–10× EBITDA; PE buyers are active. Trial period before granting equity; be wary of 50/50 partnerships.
Scaling rulesHire on demand, never emotionally. Every hire is a family you must keep busy 52 weeks/yr. Set a high standard, lead by example — a consistent core crew self-polices (near-zero leaks/call-outs).

AI Role-Play

Drill objections until your responses are automatic — practice live against a customer that fights back. Two modes, both 100% free: instant scripted drills (any device, zero setup) or open-ended local AI that runs on your own machine, no cloud and no per-message charge.

How scripted drills workPick a scenario and press Start. A customer hits you with an objection — type how you'd handle it in your own words, and you get instant coaching on which key moves you nailed (acknowledge, reassure, lead to a decision) and what to tighten, plus a strong sample answer to compare against. The wording is randomized every run, so it's never the same twice — you're always thinking on your feet. No internet or setup needed; works offline on any device.

Local AI — one-time setup (free, no cloud)

This runs a small AI model on your own computer with Ollama — no cloud, no API bill, no per-message charge. Heads up: it only works on the computer that's running Ollama (not phones), and wants a reasonably modern machine.

1
Install Ollama
Free download at ollama.com — Mac, Windows, or Linux.
2
Download a model
Open Terminal / Command Prompt and run ollama pull llama3.2 — a small, fast model. Downloads once.
3
Let this page talk to it
Ollama blocks web pages by default — allow it once. Mac: run launchctl setenv OLLAMA_ORIGINS "*", then quit & reopen Ollama. Windows: add an Environment Variable OLLAMA_ORIGINS set to *, then restart Ollama.
4
Used a different model?
Open this file in a text editor and set OLLAMA_MODEL near the bottom to match. Default is llama3.2.
