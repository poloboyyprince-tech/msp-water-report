Inside the system

Every Component, and Why We Chose It.

No mystery boxes. These are the exact parts in the Dual Tank Whole Home Filtration, what each one does to your water, and the value it adds over what most companies install.

Control valve

MSP ProValve Ceramic-Disc Control Valve

What it is
The automatic valve on top of the tank that routes every gallon: service, backwash, brine draw, rinse and refill. Instead of a piston sliding across rubber seals and plastic spacers (the design most residential softeners still use), the ProValve meters water with flat, high-hardness ceramic sealing discs.
What it does
Tracks how much water your home uses and regenerates only when the resin is actually spent, then backwashes the media and recharges it on its own. A 72-hour memory backup keeps your settings and schedule through a power outage.
Why it matters
Ceramic is harder than the sand and grit that ride along in water, so the sealing faces don't score, swell, crack or tear the way rubber seals do. Piston-style valves typically need seal-and-spacer rebuilds; a ceramic-disc valve is built to be leak-free and maintenance-free for the life of the system. MSP is one of the few residential water-treatment companies in Minnesota installing ceramic-disc valves instead of plastic and Teflon-coated piston designs.

  • Metered, on demandRegeneration

Filtration media

Jacobi AquaSorb Catalytic Coconut-Shell Carbon

What it is
Granular activated carbon made by Jacobi Carbons, steam-activated from coconut-shell charcoal and finished with a catalytic surface, no chemical impregnation. Coconut shell is a renewable agricultural by-product, which is why we specify it over coal-based carbon.
What it does
Adsorbs organic chemicals, including VOCs, pesticides and the compounds behind bad taste and odor, and catalytically breaks down chloramine and free chlorine, the disinfectants your city adds at the plant. It also reduces hydrogen sulfide. Ordinary carbon only adsorbs chloramine slowly; the catalytic surface decomposes it.
Why it matters
Jacobi's own testing shows about 65% better chloramine reduction than standard coal-based carbon after 10,000 bed volumes, and 24% better than catalytic coal carbon. Coconut-shell carbon is also harder, so it sheds less dust into your water and lasts longer in a backwashing tank. Every shower, load of laundry and glass of water gets the benefit, not just one faucet.

Softening media

USA-Made 10% Crosslinked Softening Resin

What it is
Millions of styrene-divinylbenzene ion-exchange beads. The 10% figure is the amount of divinylbenzene crosslinking that holds each bead together; most budget softeners use 8%.
What it does
As water passes through, the beads trade their sodium for the calcium and magnesium that make water hard. When capacity is used up, the valve rinses the beads with brine and they're ready again.
Why it matters
Higher crosslinking means lower moisture inside the bead (roughly 39–43% versus 43–47% for 8% resin), so chlorine can't migrate in and fracture it. Industry guidance rates 10% resin at about twice the service life of 8% under the same conditions, and it tolerates iron and daily regeneration stress better. That's the difference between resin that lasts a decade and resin that lasts two.

Underbed

Polishing Gravel Underbed

What it is
Graded quartz gravel packed beneath the media around the bottom distributor.
What it does
Supports the media bed, spreads flow evenly across the full tank diameter and keeps fine media out of the distributor screen.
Why it matters
Even flow means every bead and every grain of carbon gets contact time, so you get the full rated capacity instead of water channeling through one path. It's a small component that makes the expensive ones work.

  • Channeling, media lossPrevents
Salt storage

Brine Tank with Safety Float

What it is
A 15" × 35" salt tank with a brine well and safety float, plumbed to the control valve.
What it does
Holds the salt and makes the brine solution the valve draws in to recharge the resin.
Why it matters
The safety float stops the tank from overfilling if a valve ever sticks, and metered regeneration means salt is used only when the resin actually needs it, not on a timer.
  • 15" × 35"Size
  • Safety floatProtection
Vessel

Fiberglass-Wound Composite Mineral Tank

What it is
A polyethylene-lined, fiberglass-wound pressure vessel with a full-length riser tube and distributor.
What it does
Holds the media under household pressure and carries treated water back up the riser to the valve.
Why it matters
Corrosion-proof, rated well above municipal and well-pump pressures, and sized (12" × 52" or 10" × 54") so the media has the depth it needs for full contact time at 28 GPM.

FAQ

Questions Homeowners Ask

Do you come to my home for a consultation?
Consultations are phone calls: you pick a time online, we call you, go over your water and your home, recommend the right system at its published price and set your installation date. In-home presentations are available on request. Either way, the only required visit is the installation itself.
How much does a whole-home system cost?
Every price is published on our pricing page. Whole-home city systems run $2,999 to $4,499 and well systems $4,499 to $5,999, with a reverse osmosis drinking-water system included free. Reverse osmosis on its own is $799, a pre-sediment filter is $399 and a UV purifier is $899. Financing options are available, and travel fees may apply beyond 35 miles from Minneapolis.
What is the difference between filtration and softening?
A water softener uses ion exchange to remove calcium and magnesium hardness. Carbon filtration targets different problems, mainly chlorine and chloramine taste and odor. They solve different things, which is why our whole-home city systems combine both.
Is salt-free conditioning the same as a softener?
No. Salt-free conditioning reduces how much scale sticks to surfaces but leaves calcium and magnesium in the water. You will not get the slick soft-water feel of an ion-exchange softener. It is the right choice for homeowners who specifically want to avoid salt.
