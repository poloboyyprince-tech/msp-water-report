What it does

The proven multi-stage RO setup: purified water is produced continuously, stored in a pressurized steel tank, and delivered instantly the second you open the faucet.

  • Drinking-water Taste
  • Dissolved Solids (TDS)
  • Bottled-water Replacement

Treatment stages

  1. 01
    Multi-stage prefiltration

    Strips sediment, chlorine and dissolved contaminants before the RO membrane.

  2. 02
    RO membrane

    The RO membrane rejects 94–99% of total dissolved solids.

  3. 03
    Pressurized steel tank

    A food-grade butyl bladder inside the steel tank keeps purified water pressurized and fresh.

  4. 04
    Instant delivery

    Water is stored ready to pour: instant flow for glasses, pots and coffee makers.

FAQ

Questions Homeowners Ask

Do you come to my home for a consultation?
Consultations are phone calls: you pick a time online, we call you, go over your water and your home, recommend the right system at its published price and set your installation date. In-home presentations are available on request. Either way, the only required visit is the installation itself.
What does reverse osmosis do?
Reverse osmosis pushes water through a semipermeable membrane that rejects a broad range of dissolved solids, then delivers the treated water to a dedicated faucet at your kitchen sink. It is a point-of-use system for drinking and cooking water, not a whole-home system, which is why we pair it with whole-home treatment.
Tank RO or tankless RO — what's the difference?
A tank system slowly fills a pressurized storage tank so purified water is ready instantly. A tankless system treats water on demand, takes up far less under-sink space and has no storage tank. Both are $799 and both are included free with any whole-home system.
Does the system treat every faucet?
Whole-home systems are installed where water enters the house, so every shower, faucet and appliance receives treated water. Reverse osmosis is the exception: it treats drinking water at one dedicated faucet.
