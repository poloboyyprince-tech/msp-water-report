Common concerns

What's in Minnesota well water

Iron and manganese

Both occur naturally in Minnesota groundwater. They stain fixtures and laundry and need oxidation plus filtration.

Hydrogen sulfide

The rotten-egg smell. Oxidation converts it into a filterable form.

Sediment and microorganisms

A pre-system sediment filter protects your equipment; a UV purifier adds a disinfection barrier after filtration.

FAQ

Questions homeowners ask

How much does a whole-home system cost?
Every price is published on our pricing page. Whole-home city systems run $2,999 to $4,499 and well systems $4,499 to $5,999, with a reverse osmosis drinking-water system included free. Reverse osmosis on its own is $799, a pre-sediment filter is $399 and a UV purifier is $899. Financing options are available, and travel fees may apply beyond 35 miles from Minneapolis.
Do I need a different system for well water?
Usually, yes. City-water treatment focuses on hardness and municipal disinfectants. Private wells often also carry iron, sulfur, manganese and sediment, which need oxidation and filtration that a softener alone cannot handle. We keep the two system lines separate so you never pay for equipment your water does not need.
How much maintenance is required?
Whole-home systems backwash and regenerate themselves automatically. Your part is keeping the brine tank topped up with salt on softening systems and swapping reverse osmosis filters on schedule, which twist out by hand on the tankless unit. Well add-ons like the sediment filter and UV lamp are replaced periodically.
Should my well water be tested?
Yes, especially if you suspect iron, sulfur or manganese. Treatment depends on the form and concentration of the problem, so we go over testing on the call and set the final configuration from the results.
What warranty is included?
Whole-home and reverse osmosis systems carry a lifetime warranty on the system. We go through the exact terms with you on the phone before anything is installed.
