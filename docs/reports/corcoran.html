Your water's #1 issue

Hard Water — and Corcoran Has a Lot of It

“Hard” water just means your water carries a lot of dissolved minerals — mostly calcium and magnesium — that it soaks up from rock deep underground. Minnesota has some of the hardest water in the entire country, and Corcoran is right up there at about 22.0 grains per gallon (Extremely Hard).

Here's what hard water does around your home:

  • White, crusty buildup on faucets, showerheads, and glass
  • Spots and film on dishes and glasses, even after the dishwasher
  • Soap and shampoo that won't lather, so you use more of everything
  • Dry, itchy skin and dull, tangly hair
  • Stiff, scratchy laundry that wears out faster
  • Scale building up inside your water heater and pipes

That last one quietly costs you the most: the mineral scale makes your water heater and appliances work harder, so they wear out years sooner.

How we help: This is exactly what our Whole Home Water Filtration System is built for. It removes the hardness minerals before the water reaches any tap — so you get softer skin and hair, spot-free dishes, far less soap use, and appliances that last much longer.

Chlorine — Why Your Water Can Smell Like a Pool

Your city adds a little chlorine to your water on purpose. It's a disinfectant that kills germs and keeps the water safe on its long trip through the pipes to your home. That part is genuinely a good thing.

  • A “pool water” smell or taste, especially from the cold tap
  • Dry skin and hair after showers
  • Coffee, tea, or ice that taste a little “off”

Chlorine in city water is safe to drink — it's doing its job. Most people just don't love the taste, or what it does to their skin and hair.

How we help: Our Whole Home Water Filtration System runs your water through high-quality coconut-shell carbon that grabs the chlorine right at your house. Your water tastes and smells clean, and every shower is gentler on your skin and hair.

Saltiness Is Slowly Rising (From Road Salt)

Every winter, road salt washes off the streets into our lakes and groundwater. Year by year, that's slowly making Twin Cities water a little saltier (it's called chloride). Right now it's still at safe levels.

This isn't an emergency — it's a slow trend worth knowing about, and it can't be removed at the city plant.

How we help: If you'd like it out of your drinking water, our reverse-osmosis system takes care of it.
