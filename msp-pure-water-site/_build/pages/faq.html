FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Straight answers about water softening, filtration, reverse osmosis and well water treatment.

City-water treatment is generally focused on hardness and municipal disinfectants like chlorine and chloramine. Private well treatment may also need to address iron, sulfur, manganese, sediment and microorganisms depending on the well. MSP Pure Water separates these into dedicated system lines so you are not paying for equipment you do not need.

A traditional water softener uses ion exchange primarily to remove calcium and magnesium hardness. Carbon filtration targets different water-quality concerns such as chlorine-related taste and odor. They address different problems — many homes benefit from both.

No. Salt-free conditioning does not remove calcium and magnesium the way a traditional ion-exchange softener does. It is designed primarily to reduce scale adhesion while leaving hardness minerals in the water. You will not get the same soft-water feel as an ion-exchange softener.

Reverse osmosis pushes water through a semipermeable membrane to produce a treated drinking-water stream. It is a point-of-use treatment method used for reducing dissolved contaminants and improving drinking-water quality.

Tank systems slowly produce purified water and store it in a pressurized tank, delivering it instantly when you open the faucet. Tankless systems treat water as it is requested, use less under-sink space, and do not require a storage tank.

UV is a disinfection technology — not a general contaminant filter. It exposes water to ultraviolet light to inactivate microorganisms such as bacteria and viruses. It does not remove hardness, iron, sediment or dissolved chemicals. UV performs best after prefiltration.

A pre-system sediment filter captures physical debris like sand, silt, dirt and rust before those particles reach more expensive downstream treatment equipment like softening resin, carbon media and valves.
